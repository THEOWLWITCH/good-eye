243|<h3>👁️ Camera eye tracking — for a better fit</h3>
244|<p>When it is on, the computer or phone camera helps the app understand <b>how you actually read</b>, not only what you choose in the game:</p>
245|<li><b>📏 Distance from the screen</b>: estimated from the size of the iris (the colored part of the eye). Letter size then matches what the eye sees, even when sitting close or far.</li>
246|<li><b>🔍 Leaning in</b>: someone who moves closer while reading probably needs bigger letters. The app will offer to enlarge them.</li>
247|<li><b>🎯 Looking at the center</b>: during calibration and the distortion test it matters to look at the middle point. The camera notices when the gaze moves and asks to return to the center.</li>
248|<li><b>😴 Tiredness and effort</b>: lots of blinking or squinting. This tells when to take a break, and when game results are less reliable.</li>
249|<h4>⚠️ When it won't work, or works less well</h4>
250|<p><b>It won't work at all:</b></p>
251|<li>Inside claude.ai, which blocks the camera. It only works on the standalone Good Eye site.</li>
252|<li>When there is no camera, or you haven't allowed the browser to use it.</li>
253|<li>When the face isn't fully visible: out of the picture, head turned sideways, or a hand or mask covering the eyes.</li>
254|<li>On a very old or slow device. Measuring will be slow or stop.</li>
255|<p><b>It will work less well (the numbers are only an estimate):</b></p>
256|<li><b>Lighting:</b> a dark room, strong light behind the head (a window), or shadow on the face.</li>
257|<li><b>Glasses:</b> reflections in the lenses. Very strong glasses, such as magnifying glasses, change the size of the eye in the picture, so <b>the distance comes out wrong</b>. Calibrate the distance ("sit at 40 cm") wearing your reading glasses.</li>
258|<li><b>Distance:</b> very close, under 20 cm, or very far, over a meter.</li>
259|<li><b>Movement:</b> children who move a lot. Measuring smooths out the jitter but reacts slowly.</li>
260|<li><b>Eye conditions:</b> nystagmus (shaking eyes) and squint confuse the "looking at the center" check. A drooping eyelid or half-closed eye makes blinks hard to detect. A very light iris, for example in albinism, is harder to recognize. In these cases rely mainly on the distance measurement, or turn it off.</li>
261|<p><b>What an ordinary camera can never do:</b></p>
262|<li>Know <b>exactly which word or letter you are looking at</b>. Accuracy is roughly "center or side".</li>
263|<li>Move <b>the distortion correction together with your gaze</b>. That needs a dedicated eye tracker connected to the computer.</li>
264|<li>Diagnose. This is <b>not a medical test</b> and not a replacement for an eye doctor.</li>
